Practical Lab Overview

The practical labs are designed to turn lecture concepts into observable AI behavior.

You will not only implement algorithms. You will also predict what they should do, trace important steps, compare alternatives, analyze performance, debug incorrect approaches, and explain why particular results occur.

Across the semester, the labs progress from intelligent agents and classical search to optimization, constraint satisfaction, adversarial search, and introductory Machine Learning, Natural Language Processing, and Computer Vision.

ImportantWhat matters in the labs

A correct output is not enough.

You should be able to explain what the algorithm did, why it produced that result, and what would change if the problem, heuristic, constraint, parameter, or data changed.

How the Labs Work

A typical two-hour lab follows this progression:

  1. Objectives and recap — identify the concepts and expected behavior.
  2. Guided demonstration — examine a small worked example.
  3. Core practical task — implement or complete the main algorithmic activity.
  4. Experiment and analysis — compare configurations, parameters, or problem instances.
  5. Challenge or debugging task — modify, predict, or repair a solution.
  6. Reflection and submission — summarize the most important observation.

The exact timing may vary by lab, but the emphasis remains the same: implementation should lead to reasoning and analysis.

Lab Assessment

Lab Experiments and Exams contribute 20 marks to the course assessment.

Evidence of understanding may include:

  • correct implementation;
  • algorithmic or modeling justification;
  • experimental analysis;
  • manual traces and predictions;
  • debugging;
  • interpretation of results;
  • short individual understanding checks;
  • complete and readable submissions.

A working program can earn only part of the available credit if the underlying AI concepts cannot be explained.

NoteBe ready to explain your work

You may be asked to predict what will happen after a small change, explain a selected part of your code, interpret an unexpected result, or identify why a particular algorithmic decision was made.

Practical Tools

The main environment is:

  • Python 3
  • Jupyter Notebook
  • Python standard library
  • NumPy
  • Matplotlib
  • limited use of scikit-learn in the advanced-AI labs

In the early and core algorithmic labs, you are expected to understand and implement the important AI logic yourself rather than treating a library call as the solution.

For the advanced-AI labs, established libraries may be used so that the focus remains on the pipeline, representation, evaluation, and interpretation.

NotePractical Reminder

The purpose of the labs is not to produce the longest code.

Aim for a small, correct, reproducible solution that you can explain and evaluate.
